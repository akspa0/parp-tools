1. Input Channels — What the Model Sees

Each terrain tile is encoded as a 12-channel 512×512 image tensor. The channels are organized into three semantic groups that together resolve the fundamental ambiguity: a minimap photo alone cannot distinguish a flat green field from a hilltop meadow.

Channels 1–3
Minimap RGB
Satellite-style terrain photograph from the game client. Shows color and surface texture.
Channels 4–6
Normal Map RGB
Per-pixel surface orientation vectors. Encodes slope direction and steepness at every point.
Channel 7
WDL Height Prior
Low-resolution 17×17 coarse terrain skeleton from the game's world definition file, upsampled to 512×512.
Channel 8
Height Min Hint
Per-tile minimum elevation bound. Tells the model the floor of the height range.
Channel 9
Height Max Hint
Per-tile maximum elevation bound. Tells the model the ceiling of the height range.
Channel 10
Liquid Mask
Binary mask of where water, lava, or slime covers and hides the terrain surface below.
Channel 11
Liquid Height
Water surface elevation. The model knows terrain must be at or below this in liquid areas.
Channel 12
Object Footprint
Mask of where buildings, trees, and placed objects obscure terrain and often imply flat ground.

2. Model Architecture — 5-Level U-Net + PatchGAN

The generator is a symmetric encoder-decoder with skip connections that preserve fine detail. A separate PatchGAN discriminator enforces local realism during training, preventing the blurry output that pure regression produces.

Key V7.3 improvements: Residual conv blocks improve gradient flow at every level. Bilinear upsampling eliminates checkerboard artifacts from transposed convolutions. Hard clamp (0–1) replaces sigmoid, preserving gradients at height extremes.

3. Training Signals — 9-Component Loss Function

The model optimizes a weighted combination of nine loss terms. Each focuses on a different aspect of terrain quality, from raw pixel accuracy to high-frequency sharpness to adversarial realism.

L1 Global Height
Weight: 0.08
Per-pixel absolute error on the full-range global heightmap channel. Drives overall elevation accuracy.
L1 Local Height
Weight: 0.14
Per-pixel absolute error on the locally-normalized heightmap. Emphasizes within-tile detail.
Bounds MSE
Weight: 0.04
Mean squared error on the 4-value height bounds prediction (min, max, global min, global max).
Gradient L1
Weight: 0.10
First-order spatial derivatives (dx, dy). Penalizes incorrect terrain slopes — hillsides, ramps, inclines.
Edge (Sobel)
Weight: 0.12
Sobel-filtered edge magnitude matching. Forces correct ridgeline placement and cliff boundaries.
Laplacian
Weight: 0.12
Second-order curvature (∇²). Preserves sharp terrain features — peaks, valleys, and convexity changes.
SSIM
Weight: 0.05
Structural Similarity Index. Perceptual quality metric — luminance, contrast, and structure at window scale.
FFT Frequency
Weight: 0.08
Frequency-domain magnitude matching via log-FFT. Preserves high-frequency terrain texture.
Adversarial (GAN)
Weight: 0.12
PatchGAN discriminator signal. The critic punishes blurry patches, forcing the generator to produce sharp, realistic terrain.

7. The Goal — Reconstructing Lost Terrain

In 2010, Blizzard accidentally deployed internal development map files in a 4.0.0.12304 Cataclysm beta PTR build. The patcher began remotely deleting them, but one person noticed, stopped the updater, and saved what they could. Not everything was caught in time — 114 files were zeroed out before the patcher was stopped. That zip was kept safe for over 15 years.

The long-term goal remains full terrain reconstruction quality for damaged and missing map regions. The current state is now broader than the earlier two-root smoke: the full trusted-corpus baseline has completed across all non-quarantined collected roots through epoch 5 with an improved best validation loss.

WoW data files are essentially highly optimized SQL tables: each ADT or PM4 packs intense amounts of structured records across dozens of chunk types. The PM4 pathfinding format contains navigation meshes with terrain geometry encoded inside, but no one had made much sense of them. After 2.5 years of reverse engineering, we've decoded the chunk linkage and data structures further than anyone else in those 15 years.

Prior development map restoration efforts (2021) worked without ML and reached a ceiling. This model learns the correlation between what terrain looks like and what terrain is, but every claim here is now scoped to validated evidence from current smoke runs.

The 363 tiles with minimaps and PM4 data but no ADTs can use PM4 meshes to generate object masks — telling the model where pathfinding obstacles sit on the terrain. The WDL provides the coarse elevation anchor. Every scrap of real data we can give the model makes the predictions better.

The current output includes a historical epoch-5 best baseline plus an active fresh epoch-0 restart with measured startup-cache acceleration and stitched object-mask exports, not final map restoration signoff.
